# Cartels and the institutional record

Public-source assessment · Reviewed 2026-10-05

## Research question

What do selected public records establish about Sinaloa and CJNG, and how much do they tell us about the effectiveness of the institutional response?

Organization-level public-source research on Sinaloa and CJNG, with institutional oversight and research-method context. A purposive source sample, not a comprehensive intelligence assessment.

## Assessment

The reviewed record establishes a dated U.S. threat assessment, formal designations, sanctions, and criminal proceedings. It does not establish the organizations' present strength or the causal effectiveness of the response. Oversight findings and contested modeling show why those questions need separate evidence.

## Key judgments

### A01 — The reviewed record shows sustained U.S. institutional concern and action toward Sinaloa and CJNG across distinct legal instruments. It does not measure their current organizational strength.

An inference about this selected record, not a trend estimate. Designation, sanctions, allegations, and a reported plea have different legal meanings; the DOJ examples are different cases.

## Evidence gaps

- Have designation or sanctions listings changed since the dated actions cited here?
- What do the signed court records and later dispositions establish in the two DOJ examples?
- What independently evaluated outcomes support claims of institutional effectiveness?
- How sensitive are published academic estimates to model identification and input definitions?

Signed court records, later dispositions, and current listing changes were not independently retrieved. Selected academic full-text sections were reviewed; no model was independently reproduced. This source sample is not a comprehensive intelligence assessment.
