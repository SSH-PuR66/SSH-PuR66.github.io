Whose average is it?

People serving long sentences are more likely to appear in a prison survey. The 2025 paper reweights each supplied sentence by 1 / sentence length to approximate assignments. The code’s expected mean is 8.713 years; the unweighted extract mean is 20.085 years. That difference reproduces the sampling rule, not the paper’s policy conclusion.

What this calculation establishes

The aggregate distribution is derived from the author’s exact CSV at commit 224e9cc. Its 40,297 rows are not a cartel-member census. We retained sentence/count pairs only. No person identifier, state or offence column is published here.

The inverse-length correction assumes the observed prison stock can approximate sentence inflows after length correction. Trends in admissions, early release, mortality, survey design and offence composition can violate that approximation. These outputs are not survey-weighted national estimates. The original INEGI microdata extraction has not been independently rebuilt.

Formula: mean assigned years = Σ(n/s × αs) / Σ(n/s). Capped years replace αs with min(αs, 69 − age). The age is fixed for this slice; the full cohort’s age distribution is not reconstructed.

The headcount has an assumed scale.

The full 2023 network equation has a scale transformation that retains both conflict and saturation. For any positive k, multiply the initial population and incapacitation parameter by k, divide conflict and saturation parameters by k, and keep recruitment and the network fixed. Every population trajectory scales by k.

C′ = kC · η′ = kη · θ′ = θ/k · ω′ = ω/k
ρ′ = ρ · S′ = S · f′ = kf · g′ = kg

Substituting into each term of equation 1 gives k times the original derivative. Conflict deaths, incapacitations, recruitment and saturation totals all scale by k. With targets fT and gI also multiplied by k, the raw least-squares loss becomes k²E; relative residuals remain the same. Changing f or g while leaving every other parameter fixed is a different experiment.

For the supplied deterministic initial vector, C′(0) = kC(0). This does not map a Poisson draw to another Poisson draw, prove equality under fixed optimizer bounds or priors, or reproduce the authors’ calibration. The exact checks use rational arithmetic in 20 cases; four 208-step RK4 trajectories check the implementation.

FOLLOW THE IMPLEMENTATION

Three questions the source code leaves open.

The 2025 cohort model differs from the 2023 network model. These findings apply to the supplied 2025 R revision. A complete R execution and the impact on published figures remain unverified.

M01 / verified source inspection

Post-release calendar index stays fixed

Both release functions initialize counter at zero; the only increment is commented out. Each person therefore reuses the same calendar index inside the weekly loop.

Why it matters: The supplied loop does not advance the population-dependent hazard lookup over that person’s post-release weeks.

No full R rerun or published-effect change is established. An intentionally frozen-hazard approximation remains possible; its rationale is not established by this inspection.

M02 / verified source inspection and bounded calculation

Assigned years exceed some remaining lifetimes

Release classification uses age 69, while the displayed incarceration-saved calculation sums complete assigned sentences without that cap.

Why it matters: Section 4.5 defines the metric as summed sentence lengths, so the code follows that definition. The question is compatibility with finite-lifetime potential: assigned sentence-years and years available before age 69 differ. The explorer quantifies that difference at a chosen age.

A single chosen age is a sensitivity slice, not the paper’s age distribution, an estimate of real time served, or a corrected policy-effect result.

M03 / verified source comparison

The sampled policy domain differs from the methods domain

The methods describe sentence multipliers alpha ≥ 1; the supplied analysis samples 2 × runif(1), spanning 0 to 2. Values below 1 shorten sentences.

Why it matters: A reproducible comparison should specify the intended policy domain and include fixed alpha=1 and alpha=2 baselines.

This checks the supplied sampling domain, not the origin of every plotted point or the direction of a recomputed policy effect.
